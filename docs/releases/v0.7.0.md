## v0.7.0

### Simulate time and seeds

- **Virtual server clock (#230).** `EmbeddedServerHost.UseVirtualClock(tickMs)` makes ticks, not the wall clock, drive the server's time until the returned `VirtualClock` is disposed. Each `Tick()` moves it by exactly `tickMs`.
  - Server systems, tick listeners, delayed callbacks, `world.ElapsedMilliseconds`, the uptime and, by default, the calendar all follow it. Hunger, AI, block entity ticks and entity physics therefore advance by simulated time on any machine.
  - `clock.Advance(span)` ticks in steps of 250 ms or less, because entity physics drops time past 0.4 s.
  - Disposing goes back to the wall clock without a jump. A clock a test leaves in use ends with the test.
  - The clock is recorded in `run.json` as `serverClock`.
- **Wait for background work (#230).** `EmbeddedServerHost.WaitForBackgroundWorkAsync()` waits until relight, chunk loading and generation, random block ticks, the creature spawner and async pathfinding have no work queued. These threads keep running on real time.
- **Seeded randomness (#231).** `Host.Random` provides a seeded pseudo-random generator recorded in `run.json`. Replays with the same seed reproduce exact rolls for entity spawns, drop chances and loot.
- **Game event recording and assertions (#232).** `Host.Events` records server and client game events. Assert on ordered sequences, filters, counts and payloads without coupling tests to polling loops.
- **Server tick listeners and fast-forward (#233).**
  - `Host.TickListeners` lists the server's game tick listeners and delayed callbacks by position, entity, mod or owner type. Each one comes with its interval, when it last ran, its handler and its owner.
  - `FastForwardAsync(pos, span, step)` runs the ones at a block position on simulated time, at once. A firepit cooks and a quern grinds as far as they would in that time, without waiting real seconds, and the next tick owes them only what was left over.
  - `PharosAssert.NoTickListenersAt(host, pos)` catches listeners a block entity leaves behind when it is removed.
- See the "Virtual Clock" and "Tick Listeners and Fast-Forward" sections of `docs/writing-server-scenarios.md`.

### Player situations and vitals

- **Player rollback (#234).** Rollback restores player entity positions, inventory contents, survival vitals and world-level mod data across test scenario boundaries.
- **Player vitals (#235).** `player.Vitals` reads and changes the survival state the game keeps on the player entity: health, satiety and the five nutrient levels, oxygen, and body temperature and wetness.
  - Every value comes from the game's own behaviour (`health`, `hunger`, `breathe`, `bodytemperature`), found by the name the game gives it, so nothing depends on the shape of the watched attribute trees and what the game derives, such as max health after a modifier, is the game's own.
  - `Read()` takes a `VitalsReading` of everything at once. `Set(...)` changes only what it is named, `SetMaxHealthModifier` and `RemoveMaxHealthModifier` are what a trait or a mod does, `ConsumeSaturation` and `ReceiveSaturation` reach hunger changes without waiting ten seconds, and `WaitForAsync` ticks until a value comes.
  - Hunger is skipped in creative and spectator mode, so changing or spending it for such a player throws instead of quietly doing nothing.
  - `Player.Vitals` is the client's copy, `ServerPlayer` is the joined player's server side, and `ClientServerAssert.VitalsSynced(Session, playerName, values:)` compares them. Only health and wetness are compared by default: oxygen, air and body temperature are worked out on each side for itself, so the two drift.
  - See "Player vitals" in `docs/writing-server-scenarios.md` and "Survival state on both sides" in `docs/writing-client-server-scenarios.md`.
- **Damage, death and respawn (#236).** `player.DamageAsync(amount, damage)` goes through `Entity.ReceiveDamage`, so armour, resistances, invulnerability frames and every behaviour and mod hook run for a real hit.
  - `DamageInfo` says what happened: the damage type, what kind of thing caused it, the entity that hit, the weapon tier and the knockback, and `OverTime(duration, ticks, effect)` spreads the damage out the way a poison or a bleeding wound does.
  - `player.KillAsync()` ends the player the way `EntityPlayer.Die` does, with a death reason and `OnEntityDeath` fired. `player.RespawnAsync()` sends the packet a real client sends when its player clicks Respawn, and ticks until the player is alive again.
  - `Player.Death` is the client's side: whether the death screen is up, and `Respawn()` clicks Respawn on it with a real mouse click, so the dialog, the button and the packet all run.
  - A creative or spectator player takes no damage but healing, so the world has to be a survival one; the docs say which play style.
  - See "Damage, death and respawn" in `docs/writing-server-scenarios.md` and "Dying and coming back" in `docs/writing-client-server-scenarios.md`.
- **Mounts, boats and beds (#237).** Mount and steer horses or boats, sit on chairs, and sleep through nights in beds with full server-client packet synchronization and calendar fast-forward.
- **Headless acting players (#238).** Headless bots act in the world: navigate, jump, break blocks, place blocks, swing weapons, use items, interact with entities, and manipulate inventory slots.

### World, entities and content

- **Entity driver (#239).** `Host.Entities` spawns and configures entities on the server game thread, waits for death and despawn events or a target position, and starts, stops, and lists AI tasks.
  - `PathfindAsync` uses Vintage Story's asynchronous pathfinder and returns the route or a general no-route reason, including a one-waypoint route when already at the target.
  - `CanSpawnAt` reports local runtime light, climate, forestation, height, aquatic habitat, inside-block, or support-block failures. It does not simulate a complete natural spawn round.
  - `ClientServerAssert.EntitySynced` compares an entity's code, life state, and position. `WatchedAttributeSynced` compares one watched attribute on both sides.
  - See "Entities, AI and pathfinding" in `docs/writing-server-scenarios.md` and "State Assertions" in `docs/writing-client-server-scenarios.md`.
- **Block-entity processing (#240).** Drive and assert on firepits, cooking pots, ovens, barrels, and querns with simulated meal cooking, item heating, bread baking, liquid brewing, and grain grinding.
- **Crops, farmland and climate (#241).** Plant seeds, simulate crop growth across calendar days, inspect soil nutrients (N, P, K), moisture levels, and apply localized `ClimateOverride` regions.
- **Crafting (#242).** Test grid recipes, clayforming, knapping, and UI crafting grid inputs on both server and client without requiring a connected human player.
- **World physics (#243).** Test liquid flow and containment, falling sand/gravel physics, fire propagation, light level evaluations (sunlight and blocklight), and enclosed room recognition.
- **Land claims and PvP (#244).** Configure player roles, privilege groups, land claim boundaries, access permissions, and PvP combat rules between multiple players.

### Server lifecycle and fault injection

- **Crash and restart (#245).** Crash the server process and restart on the existing save file; verify that reconnecting clients restore state and resume simulation cleanly.
- **Save and reload round trips (#246).** Force world saves, restart from saved databases, and smoke-test save-reload fidelity with `pharos smoke --save-reload`.
- **Fault injection (#247).** Inject server-side anomalies: simulated slow ticks, throwing packet handlers, save failures (`FailSaves()`), and per-player network latency or dropped packets.
- **Soak tests (#248).** Simulate extended game days on accelerated clocks while asserting strict budgets on tick execution time, managed heap allocations, and live entity counts.
- **Record and replay (#249).** Capture input and network sessions with per-tick state hashes to detect exactly where divergent behavior begins across builds.

### Writing tests and test environments

- **Tick-based success conditions (#250).** Replace wall-clock delays with tick-based step assertions, dynamic skipping, and declarative scenarios.
- **Survival world presets and scene builder (#251).** Boot standard survival world presets (`standard`, `surviveandbuild`) with pre-warmed caches, or construct test scenes from ASCII layout strings.
- **Golden images v2 (#252).** Capture client render output with pixel-error thresholds, region exclusion masks, per-platform visual baselines, and automated baseline creation.
- **Flaky tests (#253).** Configure attempt retries, repeat-until-failure diagnostics, automatic rerun of failed tests, and structured quarantine reporting.

### Framework and tooling

- **API conventions (#254).** Unified async method naming (`*Async`), non-null typed property accessors, explicit cancellation tokens, and retirement of legacy mock abstractions.
- **Documentation and analyzers (#255).** All documentation code snippets verified by compilation tests; Roslyn analyzers detect common scenario mistakes (`PHAROS001` through `PHAROS005`).
- **CI sharding and shared hosts (#256).** Deterministic CI test sharding across parallel workers on Linux and Windows, with shared host caching to keep test matrix execution within time limits.
- **Framework-neutral scenario engine (#257).** Decoupled the scenario execution pipeline from xUnit v2 into a core engine, and shipped the preview `Zaldaryon.Pharos.XUnit.V3` package for xUnit v3 suites.
- **Transport and determinism follow-ups (#362, #401, #402).** Hardened `LinkedUdpNetClient` loopback transport for inline UDP packets, confirmed chunk persistence guarantees under save faults, and ensured seeded farming tests freeze calendar progression before client connection.
